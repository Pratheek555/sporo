## Medical and aftercare sources

- [East Kent Hospitals University NHS Foundation Trust, micro-pigmentation aftercare](https://leaflets.ekhuft.nhs.uk/micro-pigmentation-medical-tattooing-aftercare-advice/html/). The guidance says to avoid chlorinated water until the area has fully healed, keep it out of direct sunlight until fully healed and use sunblock after healing. It also advises medical review for infection concerns.
- [University College London Hospitals, tattoos and cosmetic procedures](https://www.uclh.nhs.uk/patients-and-visitors/patient-information-pages/tattoos-and-cosmetic-procedures). The page describes infection signs and advises contacting a GP or urgent service for worsening redness, swelling or fever.
- [American Academy of Dermatology, caring for tattooed skin](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/tattoos/caring-for-tattooed-skin/). The guidance recommends sunscreen on tattooed skin to limit ultraviolet fading. The drafts apply this only after the skin has healed.

The NHS pages concern medical tattooing, so these drafts use only the conservative points shared with general wound care. They do not state a universal number of days for healing, swimming, exercise or sunscreen. Readers are told to follow the written aftercare for their session and consult a qualified healthcare professional about infection or personal medical concerns.

Used for the cross-border commuter angle without adding travel-time claims.

## Tattoo aftercare

- [University College London Hospitals, Tattoos and cosmetic procedures](https://www.uclh.nhs.uk/patients-and-visitors/patient-information-pages/tattoos-and-cosmetic-procedures) advises following artist aftercare and seeking medical attention for infection signs, with urgent assessment for severe redness, swelling or fever. Used for conservative infection language.
- [East Kent Hospitals, medical tattooing aftercare](https://leaflets.ekhuft.nhs.uk/micro-pigmentation-medical-tattooing-aftercare-advice/html/) advises avoiding chlorinated water until fully healed and avoiding direct sunlight until fully healed. Used with the stricter assignment rule to avoid all swimming until full healing.
- [University Hospitals of Leicester, medical tattooing aftercare](https://yourhealth.leicestershospitals.nhs.uk/library/musculoskeletal-specialist-surgery-mss/burns-plastics/970-medical-tattooing-micropigmentation-aftercare/file) advises no swimming until fully healed and no direct sunlight until fully healed, followed by sunblock after healing. Used to support the distinction between shade or clothing during healing and sunscreen after healing.
- [NHS sunscreen and sun safety](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/seasonal-health/sunscreen-and-sun-safety/) notes that water washes sunscreen off and reflected light from water increases ultraviolet exposure. Used only for advice about sunscreen on healed skin around coastal activity.

## Medical aftercare

- [American Academy of Dermatology, Caring for tattooed skin](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-basics/tattoos/caring-for-tattooed-skin). Primary professional medical source. Recommends broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher for tattooed skin exposed to sun. The drafts apply this only after full healing and explicitly say not to put sunscreen on unhealed skin.
- [University College London Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust, Tattoos and cosmetic procedures](https://www.uclh.nhs.uk/patients-and-visitors/patient-information-pages/tattoos-and-cosmetic-procedures). Primary hospital source. Advises medical review for infection and urgent care for very red or swollen skin or fever. Supports the conservative instruction to seek medical care for spreading redness, marked swelling, worsening pain, pus or fever.
- [University Hospitals Coventry and Warwickshire NHS Trust, Tattooing service](https://www.uhcw.nhs.uk/download/clientfiles/files/Patient%20Information%20Leaflets/Surgical%20Services/Breast/Tattooing%20service.pdf). Primary hospital source. Advises avoiding swimming for four weeks after medical tattooing because chlorine may contribute to infection and fading. The drafts state the stricter outcome-based rule requested in the brief: no swimming or soaking until fully healed, without assigning a universal number of days.
